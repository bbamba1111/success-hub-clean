## 1. Purpose

The Startup Business Readiness Assessment™ helps a founder who is starting a business understand:

1. What business foundations they already have.
2. What foundations are partly developed.
3. What they have not created yet.
4. What they do not understand and need help learning.
5. What the BO$$, Cherry Blossom™, and the AI Executive Team can help them research, develop, document, and improve.

This is not a business-plan exam and it is not a pass/fail test. A founder may arrive with only an idea. The BO$$ should help turn that idea into a well-founded business and operating system while honoring the founder's chosen life and work boundaries.

The system must assess both **business readiness** and **boundary readiness**. A startup founder should not be required to experience business problems before receiving useful guidance. The objective is to build sound practices and human-sustainability boundaries into the business from the beginning.

## 2. Relationship to the two founder pathways

The BO$$ has one operating environment and two entry pathways:

- **Startup / Build:** identify what the founder already has, identify what is missing, and help build the business foundations and operating system from the beginning.
- **Established / Redesign:** understand the current business, identify operating and boundary collisions, and decide what to retain, repair, replace, redesign, systemize, automate, delegate, or eliminate.

These are pathways, not separate products or separate operating systems. A founder may use Build for one business function and Redesign for another. Do not permanently classify a founder as only a startup or established founder when the actual state can vary by business area.

The shared Reality Check sections remain shared. The Startup Business Readiness Assessment™ is the startup-specific business inventory. Startup Boundary Design™ captures the founder's intended boundaries and the operating rules to establish before launch.

## 3. Assessment status model

Every assessed business foundation must use one of these statuses:

| Status | Meaning | System treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Already in place** | The founder says the foundation exists and can identify or provide it. | Ask for its name, location, link, or artifact where useful; offer review rather than rebuilding automatically. |
| **Partly in place** | Some decisions or work exist, but the foundation is incomplete. | Ask what is complete and what remains; create a focused improvement task. |
| **Not in place yet** | The founder has not created it. | Explain why it matters and offer a guided build workflow. |
| **I don't know what this is / I'm not sure** | The founder needs explanation or discovery before they can assess it. | Teach in plain language, ask a short discovery question, and do not treat uncertainty as failure. |
| **I need help improving it** | Something exists but the founder wants it reviewed or strengthened. | Request the existing asset or facts, assess it, and propose revisions for founder approval. |
| **Not applicable to my business** | The item genuinely does not apply to this business model or stage. | Record the reason; do not count it as missing or complete. |

Do not use one blank, skipped, or unanswered state to mean all of the above. Preserve the difference between "not applicable," "not in place," "not sure," and "not answered."

An "Already in place" answer is a founder-reported status, not proof that the asset is complete, accurate, legally compliant, validated, or effective. The system may mark an asset **founder-reported**, **submitted for review**, **reviewed**, or **founder-approved** as separate evidence states.

## 4. Assessment experience and timing

**Design direction:** the assessment is a guided inventory, not a long form that blocks the founder from receiving the Work-Life Balance Reality Check™ report.

The Sunday Reality Check is a 90-minute live group experience. The startup founder should be able to complete the initial readiness snapshot before the session or continue the detailed foundation work in My Boundary Blueprint™ after the session. Do not force all detailed business-building work into the 90-minute group session.

Recommended sequence:

1. Founder selects the startup pathway.
2. Founder completes the shared Business Context questions.
3. Founder completes the short readiness snapshot below.
4. The Reality Check report is generated from the answers available at that point.
5. The BO$$ opens a persistent Startup Business Foundations workspace in My Boundary Blueprint™.
6. Cherry Blossom™ helps the founder work through missing or partially developed foundations in an order based on dependencies, founder goals, and chosen boundaries.
7. Each approved business asset is saved and becomes available to authorized BO$$ functions and AI Executives.

This is the recommended product flow. Exact pre-session/post-session placement remains an implementation decision and must be verified against the current Reality Check experience.

## 5. Readiness snapshot — core questions

Use the same status choices in Section 3 for each of the eight foundations. Ask the short question first. Reveal conditional follow-ups only when the founder selects a status that requires them.

### A. Business identity and direction

**Core question A1:** Have you defined the business you want to build and the problem it will solve?

Conditional follow-ups:

- If **Already in place**: What is the business idea, who is it intended to help, and what problem does it solve? Do you have a written business concept or description you can share?
- If **Partly in place**: Which parts have you decided: the business idea, the problem, the purpose, the business name, or how it will earn revenue?
- If **Not in place yet** or **I don't know**: What skills, experience, knowledge, interests, or products are you considering building a business around? What change would you like to help create?
- If **Need help improving it**: Share your current description. What feels unclear or incomplete?

**Foundation output:** Business Concept & Direction Brief™ — business idea, problem/opportunity, purpose, initial business model hypothesis, founder strengths, and decisions still requiring validation.

### B. Ideal client and market understanding

**Core question B1:** Have you identified the people or organizations you want to serve and researched whether they need what you plan to offer?

Conditional follow-ups:

- If **Already in place**: Describe your ideal client or customer. What specific problem do they need solved? Where do they look for help? What research or conversations support your understanding?
- If **Partly in place**: Which do you know: client type, industry, role, problem, buying motivation, budget, or where to reach them?
- If **Not in place yet** or **I don't know**: Who do you most want to help? What problem have you seen them experience? What makes you interested or qualified to help?
- If **Need help improving it**: Share your current Ideal Client Profile™ or target-market notes. What needs to be tested or clarified?
- Follow-up for all founders: Have you spoken with potential clients or customers? **Yes / No / A few conversations / Not sure how to do this.**

**Foundation output:** Ideal Client Profile™ and Market Validation Brief™. Clearly distinguish researched facts, founder assumptions, and hypotheses requiring validation. Never invent customer evidence or claim validation that has not occurred.

### C. Business message and 30-second pitch

**Core question C1:** Can you clearly explain what your business does, who it helps, and why someone would need it?

Conditional follow-ups:

- If **Already in place**: Paste or record your current introduction or 30-second pitch. Where do you use it, and how comfortable are you delivering it?
- If **Partly in place**: Which parts can you explain clearly: what you do, who you help, the problem, the outcome, or why it matters?
- If **Not in place yet** or **I don't know**: In your own words, what do you want people to understand about your business? What do you want the right person to do after hearing about it?
- If **Need help improving it**: Share your current pitch. What feels difficult to explain or remember?

**Founder education and build workflow:** Cherry Blossom™ explains what a 30-second pitch is, why it is useful, and where a founder may use it (networking, introductions, meetings, events, interviews, speaking, podcasts, and prospective-client conversations). The system helps draft and refine a natural pitch from the founder-approved business concept, ideal client, problem, offer, and value proposition. It then supports practice, rehearsal, timing, and feedback.

**Foundation output:** Business Message & 30-Second Pitch™ with a clear written version, a conversational version, and a practice version. Do not optimize for a robotic script. The founder must approve the final wording.

### D. Product, service, and offer

**Core question D1:** Have you defined the product or service you plan to sell and what a customer receives?

Conditional follow-ups:

- If **Already in place**: What is the offer called? What does the customer receive? What outcome does it help create? What is included and excluded? How is it delivered?
- If **Partly in place**: Have you decided the customer, problem, outcome, format, scope, duration, delivery method, or support included?
- If **Not in place yet** or **I don't know**: What knowledge, skill, service, product, or result could you provide? What problem might it solve?
- If **Need help improving it**: Share the current offer description or materials. What needs to be clearer, more useful, or easier to deliver?

**Foundation output:** Offer Blueprint™ — offer name, target customer, problem, promised outcome (without guarantees), components, scope, exclusions, delivery method, customer responsibilities, and open decisions.

### E. Pricing and revenue model

**Core question E1:** Have you decided how the business will earn revenue and what you will charge?

Conditional follow-ups:

- If **Already in place**: What is the price, payment structure, revenue model, estimated cost to deliver, and expected gross margin? What informed the price?
- If **Partly in place**: Which parts are decided: price, payment terms, delivery cost, revenue target, or number of sales needed?
- If **Not in place yet** or **I don't know**: What kind of payment do you think customers might make (one-time, recurring, project-based, product sale, or another model)? What costs do you expect to incur?
- If **Need help improving it**: Share your current pricing and cost assumptions. What feels uncertain?

**Foundation output:** Pricing & Revenue Model™ — pricing options, assumptions, cost-to-deliver model, revenue scenarios, and open questions. Show assumptions and calculations. Do not present a suggested price as validated or make financial guarantees. Financial, tax, and legal decisions requiring professional advice must be referred to an appropriate professional.

### F. Client attraction, marketing, and sales

**Core question F1:** Do you have a plan for how the right people will discover your business, become interested, and purchase from you?

Conditional follow-ups:

- If **Already in place**: Which channels will you use? How will you capture inquiries? What happens between first contact and purchase? What follow-up process do you use?
- If **Partly in place**: Which parts exist: marketing message, channels, website, referral process, lead capture, sales conversation, proposal, payment, or follow-up?
- If **Not in place yet** or **I don't know**: How do you imagine meeting or reaching your first customers? Which channels would feel natural and sustainable?
- If **Need help improving it**: Share your current marketing or sales process. Where do prospects get stuck or disappear?

**Foundation output:** Client Attraction & Sales Pathway™ — channel plan, message, lead capture, sales stages, follow-up sequence, and initial customer acquisition experiments. External outreach, proposals, submissions, and messages remain subject to the founder's permissions and required approval.

### G. Operations, delivery, and technology

**Core question G1:** Have you mapped how the business will deliver its product or service and manage the work behind the scenes?

Conditional follow-ups:

- If **Already in place**: What happens from purchase through delivery and closeout? Which tools, systems, records, workflows, and automations support it?
- If **Partly in place**: Which parts are documented: onboarding, scheduling, communication, delivery, payment, records, customer support, or follow-up?
- If **Not in place yet** or **I don't know**: What must happen after a customer says yes for you to deliver what you promised? Which steps repeat?
- If **Need help improving it**: Share your current workflow or tools. What creates confusion, delay, or unnecessary work?

**Foundation output:** Initial Business Operating System Map™ — client journey, core workflows, tools, records, delivery steps, operating rules, and automation opportunities. The design must fit the founder's selected work and life boundaries.

### H. Business administration and financial foundations

**Core question H1:** Have you identified the business setup, financial records, and professional requirements needed to operate responsibly?

Conditional follow-ups:

- If **Already in place**: Which items have been completed: business structure, registration, licenses/permits, business banking, bookkeeping, tax planning, contracts, insurance, and recordkeeping?
- If **Partly in place**: Which are complete, in progress, or still unknown?
- If **Not in place yet** or **I don't know**: Where will the business operate, what type of business is it, and will it sell locally, nationally, or internationally?
- If **Need help improving it**: Which area needs review or organization?

**Foundation output:** Business Administration & Financial Setup Checklist™ with jurisdiction- and industry-specific questions to verify. The BO$$ may organize research and records, but must not claim legal, tax, insurance, or financial compliance. Refer the founder to qualified professionals where needed.

## 6. Shared founder context and support

The shared Business Context & Current Business™ section also captures:

- Business name, industry, and primary product or service.
- Business stage and founder role.
- People involved, including employees and contractors.
- Revenue model and approximate revenue range, where known.
- Desired life and growth vision.
- Long-term exit vision.
- Product/service delivery model.
- AI tools and automations currently used.
- Client connection experiences.
- Support & Connection.
- Most important 90-day business goal.

**Support & Connection is not the same as team capacity or delegation.** Ask: "Who do you currently have in your corner as you build and operate your business?" Options include family, friends, mentors/advisors, coaches/consultants, entrepreneurial peers or communities, business partners, employees/contractors, professional advisors, no consistent support system, and other. Treat this as a connection and support signal, not a performance grade.

Avoid asking the founder to repeat information already captured in the shared profile. Reuse saved answers and ask only for missing, changed, or more detailed information.

## 7. Startup Boundary Design™

The startup pathway also asks the founder to define the operating boundaries they want in place before launch. This is separate from the readiness status of business foundations.

Questions:

1. What kind of life do you want this business to support?
2. How much time do you want the business to require from you on a typical workday?
3. Which days do you want to work, and which days do you want to protect for life outside the business?
4. Which responsibilities do you want to own, and which would you prefer to delegate, systematize, automate, or have AI support?
5. Which personal priorities, relationships, or experiences must your business leave room for?
6. What boundaries do you want built into the business before you begin serving customers?

Record the founder's responses as intended boundaries or design requirements. Do not assume they have already been implemented. Do not impose the 4-Hour Focused CEO Workday™ or four-day week as an unchangeable personal commitment if the founder has not agreed to it; explain the model and record the founder's chosen alignment.

## 8. How results are represented

The assessment should produce:

- A readiness inventory across the eight foundations.
- A list of assets the founder reports already having.
- A list of assets submitted for review.
- Partial foundations and the specific work remaining.
- Missing foundations.
- Concepts the founder does not yet understand.
- Not-applicable items with reasons.
- Assumptions requiring validation.
- Dependencies between foundations.
- A proposed next-build sequence.
- The founder's intended operating boundaries.
- Support and connection needs.

Do not reduce readiness to a single pass/fail score. A completion percentage may be shown only if the denominator excludes not-applicable items and clearly distinguishes founder-reported status from reviewed or approved assets. The dashboard must not imply that a founder is "ready to launch" solely because every question has an answer.

## 9. Business asset lifecycle

Each output follows a controlled lifecycle:

1. **Identified:** a need or existing asset is recorded.
2. **Researching:** an AI Executive is gathering evidence or options.
3. **Drafting:** a proposed asset is being created.
4. **Founder review:** the founder is asked to review and correct it.
5. **Approved:** the founder explicitly approves the asset or decision.
6. **In use:** the approved asset is being used in the business.
7. **Review due / needs update:** a trigger indicates that the asset may be stale or should be reconsidered.

The system must preserve versions and provenance. AI-generated drafts must not be represented as founder-approved business policy or external-facing claims.

## 10. Dependencies and build sequence

The BO$$ should recommend a sensible sequence, while allowing parallel work when dependencies permit.

Typical dependency order:

1. Business concept and problem hypothesis.
2. Ideal client and market research.
3. Value proposition and business message.
4. Product/service offer definition.
5. Pricing and revenue assumptions.
6. Client attraction and sales pathway.
7. Delivery and operating workflows.
8. Administration, financial, and risk setup.
9. Launch experiments and feedback.
10. Review and improve the operating system.

This is a default dependency map, not a rigid linear program. For example, market research and founder discovery may happen in parallel. Pricing assumptions may be explored while the offer is being shaped, but the offer scope and delivery cost must be understood before final pricing is approved.

For external research:
- Identify source, date, evidence, and limitations.
- Separate source claims from Harmony Lane™ interpretation.
- Do not fabricate market size, customer interviews, testimonials, demand, competitive data, or validation.
- Do not copy protected third-party course content or proprietary frameworks into the BO$$.

For founder authority and safety:
- Founder retains decision authority over business model, ideal client, offer, pricing, legal/financial commitments, public claims, and external communications.
- AI Executives may research, analyze, draft, prepare, and execute only within verified tool access and permissions.
- Sending, publishing, purchasing, submitting, signing, or making commitments requires the appropriate approval gate and audit record.
- Do not promise guaranteed revenue, launch success, or work-life balance outcomes.
- Do not treat a readiness result as legal, tax, accounting, or financial advice.
